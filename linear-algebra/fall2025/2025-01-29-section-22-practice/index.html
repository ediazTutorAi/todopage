2.2 Practice — Linear Transformations in Geometry

MATH 3330 · Dr. Esteban Diaz
Practice Day
Review before you start
Before diving in, recall the five families of geometric linear transformations of $\mathbb{R}^2$ from last class:
  • Scaling by $k$: matrix $kI_2$
  • Orthogonal projection onto a line $L$: matrix $\begin{pmatrix}u_1^2&u_1u_2\\ u_1u_2&u_2^2\end{pmatrix}$, $\vec{u}$ a unit vector parallel to $L$
  • Reflection about a line $L$: matrix $\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\b&-a\end{pmatrix}$, $a^2+b^2=1$
  • Rotation through $\theta$: matrix $\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta&-\sin\theta\\ \sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{pmatrix}$
  • Shear (horizontal/vertical): matrix $\begin{pmatrix}1&k\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$ or $\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\k&1\end{pmatrix}$

Practice 1
Orthogonal projection
Let $L$ be the line spanned by $\vec{w}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\3\end{pmatrix}$.

(a) Find the matrix $P$ of the orthogonal projection onto $L$.
(b) Use $P$ to find $\text{proj}_L(\vec{x})$ for $\vec{x}=\begin{pmatrix}5\\-1 \end{pmatrix}$.

Solution. With $\vec{w}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\3\end{pmatrix}$, $\vec{w}\cdot\vec{w}=1+9=10$, so $$P=\frac{1}{10}\begin{pmatrix}1&3\\3&9\end{pmatrix}.$$ For (b), $\vec{x}\cdot\vec{w}=5(1)+(-1)(3)=2$, so $$\text{proj}_L(\vec{x})=\frac{2}{10}\begin{pmatrix}1\\3\end{pmatrix} =\begin{pmatrix}0.2\\0.6\end{pmatrix}.$$
Practice 2
Reflection
Find the matrix $S$ of the reflection about the line $L$ spanned by $\vec{w}=\begin{pmatrix}4\\3\end{pmatrix}$.

Solution. First find the projection matrix: $\vec{w}\cdot\vec{w}=16+9=25$, so $$P=\frac{1}{25}\begin{pmatrix}16&12\\12&9\end{pmatrix}.$$ Then $S=2P-I_2$: $$S=\frac{1}{25}\begin{pmatrix}32&24\\24&18\end{pmatrix}-\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix} =\frac{1}{25}\begin{pmatrix}7&24\\24&-7\end{pmatrix}.$$ Check: $\left(\frac{7}{25}\right)^2+\left(\frac{24}{25}\right)^2=\frac{49+576}{625}=\frac{625}{625}=1$, as expected for a reflection matrix.
Practice 3
Rotation
(a) Write the matrix of a counterclockwise rotation through $120^\circ$ ($2\pi/3$).
(b) Apply it to $\vec{x}=\begin{pmatrix}2\\0\end{pmatrix}$.

Solution. With $\theta=120^\circ$, $\cos\theta=-\tfrac12$, $\sin\theta=\tfrac{\sqrt3}2$, so $$\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta&-\sin\theta\\\sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{pmatrix} =\begin{pmatrix}-\tfrac12&-\tfrac{\sqrt3}2\\[2pt]\tfrac{\sqrt3}2&-\tfrac12\end{pmatrix}.$$ Applying it to $\begin{pmatrix}2\\0\end{pmatrix}$ gives $$\begin{pmatrix}-\tfrac12&-\tfrac{\sqrt3}2\\[2pt]\tfrac{\sqrt3}2&-\tfrac12\end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix}2\\0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-1\\\sqrt3\end{pmatrix}.$$
Practice 4
Rotation combined with a scaling — working backwards
The matrix $M=\begin{pmatrix}-1&-\sqrt3\\\sqrt3&-1\end{pmatrix}$ represents a rotation through $\theta$ combined with a scaling by $r$. Find $r$ and $\theta$.

Solution. Here $M$ has the form $\begin{pmatrix}a&-b\\b&a\end{pmatrix}$ with $a=-1$, $b=\sqrt3$. Then $$r=\sqrt{a^2+b^2}=\sqrt{1+3}=2,$$ and $\theta$ is the polar angle of $\begin{pmatrix}a\\b\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-1\\\sqrt3 \end{pmatrix}$, which lies in the second quadrant with reference angle $60^\circ$, so $$\theta=180^\circ-60^\circ=120^\circ.$$ So $M$ represents a rotation through $120^\circ$ combined with a scaling by $2$ — a doubled version of Practice 3.
Practice 5
Shear
Apply the horizontal shear with $k=2$, matrix $\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$, to the unit square with vertices $(0,0)$, $(1,0)$, $(1,1)$, $(0,1)$. Where do the four vertices land?

Solution. Applying $\begin{pmatrix}1&2\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$ to each vertex: $$(0,0)\mapsto(0,0),\quad (1,0)\mapsto(1,0),\quad (1,1)\mapsto(3,1),\quad (0,1)\mapsto(2,1).$$ The square becomes a parallelogram with the same base and height, slid sideways — the bottom edge $(0,0)$–$(1,0)$ is unchanged, while the top edge slides right by $k=2$ (from $(0,1)$–$(1,1)$ to $(2,1)$–$(3,1)$).

Practice 7
Reflection in space
Let $V$ be the plane $x_1-2x_2+2x_3=0$, and let $\vec{x}=\begin{pmatrix}5\\1\\3\end{pmatrix}$. Find $\text{ref}_V(\vec{x})$.

Solution. The vector $\vec{v}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\-2\\2\end{pmatrix}$ is perpendicular to $V$ (its components are the coefficients of the plane's equation), with $\vec{v}\cdot\vec{v}=1+4+4=9$. Since $$\vec{x}\cdot\vec{v}=5(1)+1(-2)+3(2)=5-2+6=9=\vec{v}\cdot\vec{v},$$ the formula $\text{ref}_V(\vec{x})=\vec{x}-2\left(\frac{\vec{x}\cdot\vec{v}}{\vec{v}\cdot\vec{v}} \right)\vec{v}$ simplifies to $\vec{x}-2\vec{v}$: $$\text{ref}_V(\vec{x})=\begin{pmatrix}5\\1\\3\end{pmatrix}-2\begin{pmatrix}1\\-2\\2\end{pmatrix} =\begin{pmatrix}3\\5\\-1\end{pmatrix}.$$
