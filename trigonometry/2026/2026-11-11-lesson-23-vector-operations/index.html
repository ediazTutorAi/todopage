Vector Operations
Vector Components, Direction, and Magnitude
DEFINITION: Position Vectors, Vector Components, Direction Angle and Magnitude

A vector \(\mathbf{v}\) with initial point (tail) placed at the origin and terminal point (head) at the coordinate \((a, b)\) is called a position vector, and is denoted as \(\langle a, b\rangle\).

The real numbers \(a\) and \(b\) are the horizontal and vertical components, respectively, of the vector \(\mathbf{v}\).

The positive angle formed with initial side along the positive \(x\)-axis and terminal side formed by the vector \(\mathbf{v}\) is called the direction angle. This angle satisfies the equation \(\tan\theta = \dfrac{b}{a},\ a \neq 0\).

The magnitude or length of the vector \(\mathbf{v} = \langle a, b\rangle\) is denoted as \(|\mathbf{v}|\) and can be calculated by the equation, \(|\mathbf{v}| = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}\).

Example 1
Magnitude and direction angle

Example 1. Let \(\mathbf{v} = \langle -3, -5\rangle\). Graph \(\mathbf{v}\), evaluate \(|\mathbf{v}|\), and find the direction angle.

Magnitude\(|\mathbf{v}| = \sqrt{(-3)^2 + (-5)^2} = \sqrt{34}\)
\(\tan\theta\)\(\tan\theta = \dfrac{-5}{-3} = \dfrac{5}{3}\)
Reference angle\(\theta' = \tan^{-1}\left(\dfrac{5}{3}\right) \approx 59.04^\circ\)
Direction angle\(\theta = \tan^{-1}\left(\dfrac{5}{3}\right) + 180^\circ \approx 239.04^\circ\)
Finding Vector Components
Example 2
IMPORTANT INFO: Finding Vector Components

A vector \(\mathbf{v}\), with magnitude \(|\mathbf{v}|\) and direction angle \(\theta\), has horizontal and vertical components given by the following:

\(\sin\theta = \dfrac{b}{|\mathbf{v}|} \ \to\ \) \(b = |\mathbf{v}|\sin\theta\) (vertical component)

\(\cos\theta = \dfrac{a}{|\mathbf{v}|} \ \to\ \) \(a = |\mathbf{v}|\cos\theta\) (horizontal component)

\(\mathbf{v} = \langle a, b\rangle = \) \(\langle |\mathbf{v}|\cos\theta,\ |\mathbf{v}|\sin\theta\rangle\)

(a) Vector \(\mathbf{u}\) with magnitude 7 and direction angle of \(60^\circ\).\(\mathbf{u} = \langle 7\cos(60^\circ), 7\sin(60^\circ)\rangle = \left\langle \dfrac{7}{2}, \dfrac{7\sqrt{3}}{2}\right\rangle\)
(b) Vector \(\mathbf{v}\) with magnitude 5 and direction angle of \(222^\circ\).\(\mathbf{v} = \langle 5\cos(222^\circ), 5\sin(222^\circ)\rangle \approx \langle -3.72, -3.35\rangle\)
(c) Vector \(\mathbf{w}\) with magnitude \(2\pi\) and direction angle of \(180^\circ\).\(\mathbf{w} = \langle 2\pi\cos(180^\circ), 2\pi\sin(180^\circ)\rangle = \langle -2\pi, 0\rangle\)
Vector Operations
Addition, subtraction, and scalar multiplication
DEFINITION: Vector Addition, Subtraction, and Scalar Multiplication

Let \(\mathbf{v} = \langle a, b\rangle\) and \(\mathbf{w} = \langle c, d\rangle\); we define vector addition and subtraction as follows.

\[\mathbf{v} \pm \mathbf{w} = \langle a, b\rangle \pm \langle c, d\rangle = \text{ }\]\(\langle a \pm c,\ b \pm d\rangle\)

Let \(k\) be a real value; we define scalar multiplication as follows.

\[k\mathbf{v} = k\langle a, b\rangle = \text{ }\]\(\langle ka,\ kb\rangle\)

Example 3. Let \(\mathbf{v} = \langle 1, -2\rangle\) and \(\mathbf{w} = \langle -2, 3\rangle\). For each expression, state whether the result will be a scalar or vector quantity and evaluate.

\(-2\mathbf{v}\)\(-2\langle 1, -2\rangle = \langle -2, 4\rangle\), vector
\(-2\mathbf{v} - \mathbf{w}\)\(\langle -2, 4\rangle - \langle -2, 3\rangle = \langle 0, 1\rangle\), vector
\(|-2\mathbf{v} - \mathbf{w}|\)\(|\langle 0, 1\rangle| = \sqrt{0^2 + 1^2} = 1\), scalar

Standard Basis Vectors
Example 5
DEFINITION: Unit Vector and Standard Basis Vectors

A unit vector is any vector with magnitude 1. Unit vectors are often used to indicate direction.

There are two convenient unit vectors, \(\hat{\imath} = \langle 1, 0\rangle\) and \(\hat{\jmath} = \langle 0, 1\rangle\), read aloud as \(i\)-hat and \(j\)-hat, and are sometimes referred to as the standard basis vectors.

These unit vectors are perpendicular (or orthogonal) and form a standard basis for the \(xy\)-plane. That is, any vector in the plane can be expressed as a combination of these two perpendicular unit vectors, \(\hat{\imath}\) and \(\hat{\jmath}\).

Example 5. Let \(\mathbf{v} = \langle 1, -2\rangle\) and \(\mathbf{w} = \langle -2, 3\rangle\).

(a) Express each vector \(\mathbf{v}\) and \(\mathbf{w}\) as a combination of the standard basis vectors, \(\hat{\imath}\) and \(\hat{\jmath}\). (b) Express each quantity below as a combination of standard basis vectors, \(\hat{\imath}\) and \(\hat{\jmath}\).

(a) \(\mathbf{v}\)\(\langle 1, -2\rangle = 1\langle 1, 0\rangle - 2\langle 0, 1\rangle = \hat{\imath} - 2\hat{\jmath}\)
(a) \(\mathbf{w}\)\(\langle -2, 3\rangle = -2\hat{\imath} + 3\hat{\jmath}\)
(b) \(-2\mathbf{v}\)\(-2(\hat{\imath} - 2\hat{\jmath}) = -2\hat{\imath} + 4\hat{\jmath}\)
(b) \(-2\mathbf{v} - \mathbf{w}\)\((-2\hat{\imath} + 4\hat{\jmath}) - (-2\hat{\imath} + 3\hat{\jmath}) = \hat{\jmath}\)
The Dot Product
Algebraic definition

Unlike scalar multiplication, the dot product is applied to two vector quantities and produces a scalar quantity.

DEFINITION: The Dot Product (Algebraic Definition)

Given two nonzero vectors, \(\mathbf{v} = \langle a, b\rangle\) and \(\mathbf{w} = \langle c, d\rangle\), the dot product of \(\mathbf{v}\) and \(\mathbf{w}\) is defined as follows.

\[\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{w} = \langle a, b\rangle \cdot \langle c, d\rangle = \text{ }\]\(ac + bd\)

Example 6.

(a) Let \(\mathbf{v} = \langle 1, -2\rangle\) and \(\mathbf{w} = \langle -2, 3\rangle\), evaluate \(\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{w}\).\(1(-2) + (-2)(3) = -2 - 6 = -8\)
(b) Evaluate \(\hat{\imath}\cdot\hat{\jmath}\).\(\langle 1, 0\rangle \cdot \langle 0, 1\rangle = 1(0) + 0(1) = 0\)
Important resultA zero dot product means the vectors are orthogonal.
The Dot Product
Geometric definition
DEFINITION: The Dot Product (Geometric Definition)

Given two nonzero vectors, \(\mathbf{v} = \langle a, b\rangle\) and \(\mathbf{w} = \langle c, d\rangle\), the dot product of \(\mathbf{v}\) and \(\mathbf{w}\) is defined as follows.

\[\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{w} = |\mathbf{v}||\mathbf{w}|\cos(\theta)\]

Here, \(\theta\) is the angle formed between \(\mathbf{v}\) and \(\mathbf{w}\), with \(0^\circ \le \theta \le 180^\circ\).

Example 7.

(a) Evaluate \(\hat{\imath}\cdot\hat{\jmath}\).\(|\langle 1,0\rangle||\langle 0,1\rangle|\cos(90^\circ) = (1)(1)(0) = 0\), perpendicular (orthogonal) vectors
(b) Let \(\mathbf{v} = \langle 1, -2\rangle\) and \(\mathbf{w} = \langle -2, 3\rangle\). Magnitudes\(|\mathbf{v}| = \sqrt{1^2 + (-2)^2} = \sqrt{5}\), \(|\mathbf{w}| = \sqrt{(-2)^2 + 3^2} = \sqrt{13}\)
Angle between \(\mathbf{v}\) and \(\mathbf{w}\)\(\cos\theta = \dfrac{\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{w}}{|\mathbf{v}||\mathbf{w}|} = \dfrac{-8}{\sqrt{5}\sqrt{13}}\), \(\theta = \cos^{-1}\left(\dfrac{-8}{\sqrt{65}}\right) \approx 172.87^\circ\)
