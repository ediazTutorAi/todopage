Angles in the Coordinate Plane

Trigonometry · Lesson 04
Standard Position
Standard position, quadrantal, and coterminal angles

An angle is said to be in standard position if the vertex is positioned at the origin with the initial side along the positive \(x\)-axis.

Angles formed by clockwise rotation are negative in measure, while angles formed by counterclockwise rotation are positive in measure.

Any two angles in standard position that share a common terminal side are said to be coterminal angles. Coterminal angles differ by a multiple of \(360°\).

Any angle, in standard position, with terminal side along either the \(x\)- or \(y\)-axis is called a quadrantal angle.

Demonstration
A positive and a negative angle, coterminal

\(\alpha\) and \(\beta\) share the same terminal side in QII — \(\alpha\) reaches it by sweeping \(150°\) counterclockwise (so \(\alpha > 0\)); \(\beta\) reaches the same ray by sweeping the rest of the way around clockwise (so \(\beta < 0\)). They're coterminal, differing by exactly \(360°\): \(\alpha = 150°\), \(\beta = 150° - 360° = -210°\).

Example 1
Finding coterminal angles

Find two coterminal angles, one negative and one positive, for each angle \(\theta\).

(a) \(\theta = 260°\)

\(260° - 360° = \) \(-100°\)     \(260° + 360° = \) \(620°\)

(b) \(\theta = -944°\)

\(-944° + 360° = \) \(-584°\) \(\ \to\ \) \(-584° + 360° = \) \(-224°\) \(\ \to\ \) \(-224° + 360° = \) \(136°\)

Coterminal angles: \(-224°\) and \(136°\)

Example 2
Sine, cosine, tangent for a point in QI

Let \(\theta\) be a positive acute angle in standard position, whose terminal side passes through \((x_1, y_1)\). Construct a right triangle with base along the positive \(x\)-axis, and let \(r\) be the distance from the origin to \((x_1, y_1)\).

\(r = \) \(\sqrt{x_1^2 + y_1^2}\)

\(\sin(\theta) = \) \(\dfrac{y_1}{r}\)     \(\cos(\theta) = \) \(\dfrac{x_1}{r}\)     \(\tan(\theta) = \) \(\dfrac{y_1}{x_1}\)

Definitions: Sine, Cosine, and Tangent for any Angle
Not just acute angles of a right triangle

Let \(\theta\) be any angle in standard position with terminal side passing through the point \(P(x,y)\), and distance from the origin given by \(r\), where \(r = \) \(\sqrt{x^2+y^2}\), \(r > 0\). Then:

\(\sin(\theta) = \dfrac{y}{r}\)\(\cos(\theta) = \dfrac{x}{r}\)\(\tan(\theta) = \dfrac{y}{x},\ x \ne 0\)

This works in any quadrant — our right-triangle-only definitions from Lesson 03 become inadequate once \(\theta > 90°\), but these ratios hold everywhere.

Example 4
Quadrantal angles

(a) Terminal side of \(\theta\) passing through \((-\pi, 0)\) — so \(\theta = 180°\), \(x=-\pi,\ y=0,\ r=\pi\).

\(\sin\theta = \) \(0\)     \(\cos\theta = \) \(-1\)     \(\tan\theta = \) \(0\)

(b) \(\theta = 270°\) — any point on the terminal side works, e.g. \((0,-1)\).

\(\sin\theta = \) \(-1\)     \(\cos\theta = \) \(0\)     \(\tan\theta = \) undefined

The ASTC Mnemonic
Remembering signs by quadrant

We use the mnemonic All Students Take Calculus to recall the sign of each trig function by quadrant.

Students (QII)
\(\sin\theta > 0\)
\(\cos\theta < 0,\ \tan\theta < 0\)
All (QI)
\(\sin\theta > 0,\ \cos\theta > 0,\ \tan\theta > 0\)
Take (QIII)
\(\tan\theta > 0\)
\(\sin\theta < 0,\ \cos\theta < 0\)
Calculus (QIV)
\(\cos\theta > 0\)
\(\sin\theta < 0,\ \tan\theta < 0\)
Example 6
Determine the sign

\(\sin(190°)\), \(190° \in\) QIII \(\Rightarrow\) \(< 0\)     \(\cos(337°)\), \(337° \in\) QIV \(\Rightarrow\) \(> 0\)

\(\tan(122°)\), \(122° \in\) QII \(\Rightarrow\) \(< 0\)     \(\tan(270°) = \) undefined (\(270°\) is quadrantal)

Example 7
Identify the possible quadrant(s)

(a) \(\sin(\theta) > 0,\ \cos(\theta) < 0\)

\(\theta \in\) QII

(b) \(\tan(\theta) > 0,\ \cos(\theta) > 0,\ \sin(\theta) < 0\)

No such \(\theta\) exists! (\(\cos>0\) needs QI/QIV; \(\sin<0\) needs QIII/QIV; \(\tan>0\) needs QI/QIII — no quadrant satisfies all three.)

Definitions: Reciprocal Ratios
Cosecant, secant, and cotangent for any angle
\(\sin(\theta) = \dfrac{y}{r}\)→\(\csc(\theta) = \) \(\dfrac{r}{y}\)
\(\cos(\theta) = \dfrac{x}{r}\)→\(\sec(\theta) = \) \(\dfrac{r}{x}\)
\(\tan(\theta) = \dfrac{y}{x}\)→\(\cot(\theta) = \) \(\dfrac{x}{y}\)
Example 8
Reciprocal ratios and quadrant reasoning

(a) Using the Example 3 triangle, \((-12,-5)\), \(r = 13\):

\(\csc(\theta) = \) \(-\dfrac{13}{5}\)     \(\sec(\theta) = \) \(-\dfrac{13}{12}\)     \(\cot(\theta) = \) \(\dfrac{12}{5}\)

(b) Sign of \(\sin(25°) \cdot \cot(100°) \cdot \sec(225°)\):

\((+)(-)(-) = \) positive

(c) Quadrant(s) where \(\sin(\theta) > 0\) and \(\cot(\theta) < 0\):

\(\theta \in\) QII
