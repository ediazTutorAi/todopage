The Law of Cosines
Derivation

The Law of Cosines can be developed by combining two familiar topics, the Pythagorean theorem and right triangle trigonometry.

Example 1. Complete each step outlined below to derive the rule for the Law of Cosines.

(a) (eqn. 1)\(\cos(A) = \) \(\dfrac{x}{c}\)
(eqn. 2)\(h^2 = \) \(c^2 - x^2\)
(eqn. 3)\(a^2 = \) \((b - x)^2 + h^2 = b^2 - 2bx + x^2 + h^2\)
(b) Substitute (eqn. 2) into (eqn. 3)\(a^2 = b^2 - 2bx + x^2 + (c^2 - x^2) = b^2 - 2bx + c^2\)
(c) Solve (eqn. 1) for \(x\)\(x = c\cos(A)\)
Substitute into (b)\(a^2 = b^2 - 2b\,(c\cos(A)) + c^2 = b^2 + c^2 - 2bc\cos(A)\)
Theorem: The Law of Cosines

THEOREM: Law of Cosines

For any triangle \(ABC\), with side measures \(a\), \(b\), and \(c\), we have the following relationships.

\[a^2 = b^2 + c^2 - 2bc\cos(A)\] \[b^2 = a^2 + c^2 - 2ac\cos(B)\] \[c^2 = a^2 + b^2 - 2ab\cos(C)\]
Applying the Law of Cosines
Example 2

Example 2. Solve the triangle \(ABC\) given \(A = 62.5^\circ\), \(b = 12\) cm, and \(c = 17\) cm.

We know\(A = 62.5^\circ\), \(b = 12\), \(c = 17\)
We need\(a\), \(B\), \(C\)
Find \(a\)\(a^2 = b^2 + c^2 - 2bc\cos(A) = 12^2 + 17^2 - 2(12)(17)\cos(62.5^\circ) \approx 244.61\), \(a \approx 15.64\) cm
Find \(B\)\(b^2 = a^2 + c^2 - 2ac\cos(B)\), \(\cos(B) = \dfrac{b^2 - a^2 - c^2}{-2ac} = \dfrac{12^2 - (15.6399)^2 - 17^2}{-2(15.6399)(17)} \approx 0.7327\), \(B = \cos^{-1}(0.7327) \approx 42.89^\circ\)
Find \(C\)\(C = 180^\circ - 62.5^\circ - 42.89^\circ \approx 74.61^\circ\)
Example 3
Two ships and a lighthouse

Example 3. Two ships are anchored near a lighthouse. One is located 5 km from the lighthouse while the other is 3 km away. The lighthouse beacon shines its light on one ship and then the other exactly 20 seconds later. If the beacon rotates at a rate of one revolution per minute, how far apart are these two ships?

Rotation\(\dfrac{360^\circ}{1\text{ min}}\), so in 20 seconds \(\theta = 120^\circ\)
We know\(C = 120^\circ\) (the lighthouse), \(a = 5\), \(b = 3\)
We need\(c\)
Law of Cosines\(c^2 = a^2 + b^2 - 2ab\cos(C) = 5^2 + 3^2 - 2(5)(3)\cos(120^\circ) = 49\)
Answer\(c = 7\): the ships are 7 km apart.

THEOREM: The Triangle Inequality (for a Proper Triangle)

In a proper triangle, the sum of the lengths of any two sides must be greater than the length of the third remaining side. That is, if \(a\), \(b\), and \(c\) are any three sides of a triangle, then \(a + b > c\).

Heron's Formula for the Area of a Triangle

THEOREM: Heron's Formula

For any triangle \(ABC\), with side measures \(a\), \(b\), \(c\), and semiperimeter

\[s = \frac{1}{2}(a + b + c),\]

the area of the triangle is given by the following formula.

\[\text{Area} = \sqrt{s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)}\]

Example 5. Revisit Example 4 and find the area of the resulting triangle.

We know\(a = 6\) in, \(b = 3\) in, \(c = 5\) in
Semiperimeter\(s = \dfrac{1}{2}(6 + 3 + 5) = 7\)
Area\(\sqrt{7(7-6)(7-3)(7-5)} = \sqrt{7(1)(4)(2)} = \sqrt{56} \approx 7.48\) in\(^2\)

Example 6. A triangular-shaped field is bordered on two sides by a fence and on the third side by a relatively straight river. If the length along the river is 1257 ft and the fencing length is 1020 ft along one side and 1015 ft along the other, determine the acreage of the field. Round your answer to the nearest tenth of an acre (1 acre = 43,560 square feet).

We know\(a = 1257\) ft, \(b = 1020\) ft, \(c = 1015\) ft
Semiperimeter\(s = \dfrac{1}{2}(1257 + 1020 + 1015) = 1646\)
Area\(\sqrt{1646(1646-1257)(1646-1020)(1646-1015)} = \sqrt{1646(389)(626)(631)} \approx 502{,}911.5\) ft\(^2\)
Acreage\(\dfrac{502{,}911.5}{43{,}560} \approx 11.5\) acres
