Introduction to Right Triangle Ratios
Identifying the sides

In any right triangle, there will always be two acute angles. (Why?) We select one of these angles as a reference, \(\theta\). Then we can identify the side opposite this angle as the opposite side. The side across from the right angle is always called the hypotenuse. This leaves the remaining side, next to the referenced angle, as our adjacent side.

Definitions: Sine, Cosine, and Tangent
Right-triangle based definitions
Sine\(\sin(\theta) = \dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}\)
Cosine\(\cos(\theta) = \dfrac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}}\)
Tangent\(\tan(\theta) = \dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}\)

Note: there are three other (reciprocal) ratios possible — we'll save these for later in this lesson.

We can use the acronym, SOH-CAH-TOA, to help us recall the above ratios.

Definitions: Reciprocal Ratios
Cosecant, secant, and cotangent
\(\sin(\theta) = \dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}\)→\(\csc(\theta) = \) \(\dfrac{\text{hypotenuse}}{\text{opposite}}\)
\(\cos(\theta) = \dfrac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}}\)→\(\sec(\theta) = \) \(\dfrac{\text{hypotenuse}}{\text{adjacent}}\)
\(\tan(\theta) = \dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}\)→\(\cot(\theta) = \) \(\dfrac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{opposite}}\)

Example 6
Standard position in the coordinate plane

Plot the coordinate \((5,3)\). With this coordinate and the origin as vertices, construct a right triangle with base along the positive \(x\)-axis. Let \(\theta\) be the positive (counterclockwise) acute angle from the positive \(x\)-axis to the side formed by the origin and the given coordinate.

\(\sqrt{34}\)

\(\sin\theta = \) \(\dfrac{3}{\sqrt{34}}\)     \(\tan\theta = \) \(\dfrac{3}{5}\)

The line through the origin and \((5,3)\) is \(y = \dfrac{3}{5}x\). Notice anything about the slope? The slope equals \(\tan\theta\)!

Example 7 (b)
Reciprocal ratios in the coordinate plane

Consider the right triangle in quadrant I from Example 6, with vertex \((5,3)\).

\(\csc\theta = \) \(\dfrac{\sqrt{34}}{3}\)     \(\cot\theta = \) \(\dfrac{5}{3}\)

Looking ahead: any angle formed this way — from the positive \(x\)-axis to a ray through the origin — is said to be in standard position. We'll fully develop this idea in an upcoming lesson.
