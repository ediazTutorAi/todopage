Stokes' Theorem: Examples

Stokes’ theorem
Theorem 6.19
Theorem 6.19: Stokes’ theorem

Let \(S\) be a piecewise smooth oriented surface with a boundary that is a simple closed curve \(C\) with positive orientation. If \(\vec F\) is a vector field with component functions that have continuous partial derivatives on an open region containing \(S\), then $$\oint_C\vec F\cdot d\vec r=\iint_S\operatorname{curl}\vec F\cdot d\vec S.$$

Positive orientation of C

Let \(S\) have unit normal vector \(\vec N\). The orientation of \(S\) induces the positive orientation of \(C\) if, as you walk in the positive direction around \(C\) with your head pointing in the direction of \(\vec N\), the surface is always on your left.

Surface independence (Equation 6.23)

If \(S_1\) and \(S_2\) are smooth surfaces with the same boundary \(C\) and the same orientation, then $$\iint_{S_1}\operatorname{curl}\vec F\cdot d\vec S=\oint_C\vec F\cdot d\vec r=\iint_{S_2}\operatorname{curl}\vec F\cdot d\vec S.$$

Does it work? Verifying Stokes’ theorem
Example 6.73 and Checkpoint 6.61

Example 6.73: verify Stokes’ theorem for \(\vec F(x,y,z)=\langle y,\,2z,\,x^2\rangle\) and \(S\) the paraboloid \(z=4-x^2-y^2\), \(z\ge0\), outward oriented. Compute both sides, then press the button. Green arrows are \(\vec N\), orange arrows are \(\vec F\), the black curve is \(C\).

Surface side. \(\operatorname{curl}\vec F\)\(\operatorname{curl}\vec F=\langle R_y-Q_z,\ P_z-R_x,\ Q_x-P_y\rangle=\langle-2,\,-2x,\,-1\rangle\)
The normal: \(\vec r(x,y)=\langle x,y,4-x^2-y^2\rangle\), so \(\vec t_x\times\vec t_y\)\(\langle-g_x,-g_y,1\rangle=\langle2x,2y,1\rangle\) (upward, which is the outward orientation here)
The surface integral over \(D:\ x^2+y^2\le4\)\(\displaystyle\iint_D\langle-2,-2x,-1\rangle\cdot\langle2x,2y,1\rangle\,dA=\iint_D(-4x-4xy-1)\,dA=-\text{area}(D)=-4\pi\) (the odd terms integrate to \(0\))
Line side. Parameterize \(C\) with the positive orientation (counterclockwise seen from above).\(\vec r(t)=\langle2\cos t,\,2\sin t,\,0\rangle\), \(0\le t\le2\pi\)
\(\vec F(\vec r(t))\cdot\vec r'(t)\)\(\langle2\sin t,\,0,\,4\cos^2t\rangle\cdot\langle-2\sin t,\,2\cos t,\,0\rangle=-4\sin^2t\)
The line integral\(\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi}-4\sin^2t\,dt=-4\pi\). Both sides equal \(-4\pi\): Stokes’ theorem is verified.
Checkpoint 6.61. Verify Stokes’ theorem for \(\vec F=\langle y,x,-z\rangle\) and \(S\) the upwardly oriented portion of the graph of \(f(x,y)=x^2y\) over the triangle with vertices \((0,0)\), \((2,0)\), \((0,2)\).\(\operatorname{curl}\vec F=\langle0,0,1-1\rangle=\vec 0\), so the surface side is \(0\). \(\vec F=\nabla(xy-z^2/2)\) is conservative, so the line integral around the closed curve \(C\) is \(0\) too.
Surface integral to line integral
Example 6.74, surface independence and Checkpoint 6.62

Example 6.74: calculate \(\iint_S\operatorname{curl}\vec F\cdot d\vec S\), where \(S\) is the surface on the left (a tube closed by a rounded end, open at the top) oriented outward, and \(\vec F=\langle z,\,2xy,\,x+y\rangle\).

\(S\): too complicated to parameterize.

\(S'\): the flat disk with the same boundary \(C\).

Can we use \(\iint_S\operatorname{curl}\vec F\cdot(\vec t_u\times\vec t_v)\,dA\) directly?It needs a parameterization of \(S\), which is extremely difficult here. The boundary \(C\), however, is just a circle of radius 1.
By Stokes’ theorem, the integral equals \(\oint_C\vec F\cdot d\vec r\). Parameterize \(C\) and compute \(\vec F\cdot\vec r'\).\(C:\ \vec r(t)=\langle\cos t,\sin t,1\rangle\). \(\vec F(\vec r(t))=\langle1,\,2\sin t\cos t,\,\cos t+\sin t\rangle\), so \(\vec F\cdot\vec r'=-\sin t+2\sin t\cos^2t\).
The value\(\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi}(-\sin t+2\sin t\cos^2t)\,dt=0\). (Reversing the orientation of \(C\) only changes the sign of \(0\).)
The flat disk \(S'\) has the same boundary and the same orientation. What is \(\iint_{S'}\operatorname{curl}\vec F\cdot d\vec S\)?Also \(0\): by Stokes’ theorem it equals the same line integral \(\oint_C\vec F\cdot d\vec r\).
Surface independence (Equation 6.23)

Let \(S_1\) and \(S_2\) be smooth surfaces with the same boundary \(C\) and the same orientation. Then $$\iint_{S_1}\operatorname{curl}\vec F\cdot d\vec S=\oint_C\vec F\cdot d\vec r=\iint_{S_2}\operatorname{curl}\vec F\cdot d\vec S.$$ Flux integrals of curl fields are surface independent in the same way that line integrals of gradient fields are path independent.

If \(\vec G=\operatorname{curl}\vec F\), on what does \(\iint_S\vec G\cdot d\vec S\) depend?Only on the boundary of \(S\), not on the surface itself.
\(S\) is a closed surface (an ellipsoid, say) and \(\vec F\) has continuous partial derivatives. What is \(\iint_S\operatorname{curl}\vec F\cdot d\vec S\)?\(0\): a closed surface has no boundary curve. (Cut it along a curve into two pieces with opposite induced orientations on \(C\): the two line integrals cancel.)
Checkpoint 6.62. \(\vec F=\langle z,x,y\rangle\). \(S\) is a vase lying on its side, whose open circular end is the unit circle in the \(xz\)-plane centered at the origin. \(C\) is oriented clockwise when looking along the positive \(y\)-axis. Find \(\iint_S\operatorname{curl}\vec F\cdot d\vec S\).\(\operatorname{curl}\vec F=\langle1,1,1\rangle\). Replace \(S\) by the flat disk \(x^2+z^2\le1\), \(y=0\). Clockwise looking along \(+y\) means \(\vec N=\mathbf j\). So the integral is \(\langle1,1,1\rangle\cdot\mathbf j\cdot\pi=\pi\).
Line integral to surface integral
Example 6.75 and Checkpoint 6.63

Example 6.75: calculate \(\oint_C\vec F\cdot d\vec r\), where \(\vec F=\langle xy,\ x^2+y^2+z^2,\ yz\rangle\) and \(C\) is the boundary of the parallelogram with vertices \((0,0,1)\), \((0,1,0)\), \((2,1,-2)\), \((2,0,-1)\), taken counterclockwise seen from above (upward \(\vec N\)).

Directly, how many line integrals would we compute?Four, one for each side, each with its own parameterization. Not complicated, but time-consuming.
Use Stokes’ theorem instead. Which surface \(S\) has \(C\) as its boundary?The parallelogram itself: the portion of \(z=1-x-y\) over \(0\le x\le2\), \(0\le y\le1\). Parameterization \(\langle x,y,1-x-y\rangle\).
\(\operatorname{curl}\vec F\)\(\langle R_y-Q_z,\ P_z-R_x,\ Q_x-P_y\rangle=\langle-z,\,0,\,x\rangle\)
\(\vec t_x\times\vec t_y\), and the integrand \(\operatorname{curl}\vec F\cdot(\vec t_x\times\vec t_y)\)\(\langle1,1,1\rangle\), and \(\langle-(1-x-y),0,x\rangle\cdot\langle1,1,1\rangle=2x+y-1\).
The line integral\(\displaystyle\oint_C\vec F\cdot d\vec r=\int_0^2\!\!\int_0^1(2x+y-1)\,dy\,dx=3\)
Checkpoint 6.63. \(\vec F=\langle z,x,y\rangle\), \(C\) oriented clockwise, the boundary of the triangle with vertices \((0,0,1)\), \((3,0,-2)\), \((0,1,2)\). Find \(\oint_C\vec F\cdot d\vec r\).\(\operatorname{curl}\vec F=\langle1,1,1\rangle\). The triangle is \(z=1-x+y\) over the triangle \((0,0)\), \((3,0)\), \((0,1)\); upward \(\vec t_x\times\vec t_y=\langle1,-1,1\rangle\), so the integrand is \(1\) and the area is \(3/2\). Counterclockwise would give \(3/2\); clockwise gives \(-3/2\).

Exercises 331 and 332

Exercise 331: \(\vec F=\langle2y,\,-6z,\,3x\rangle\), \(S\) the part of the paraboloid \(z=4-x^2-y^2\) above the \(xy\)-plane, upward. Compute the flux of the curl and the circulation around \(C\).

\(\operatorname{curl}\vec F\) for \(\vec F=\langle2y,\,-6z,\,3x\rangle\)\(\langle R_y-Q_z,\ P_z-R_x,\ Q_x-P_y\rangle=\langle6,\,-3,\,-2\rangle\)
Surface side. The integrand for the upward graph \(z=s(x,y)\): \(\operatorname{curl}\vec F\cdot\langle-s_x,-s_y,1\rangle\)\(\langle6,-3,-2\rangle\cdot\langle2x,2y,1\rangle=12x-6y-2\)
The surface integral over \(D:\ x^2+y^2\le4\)\(\displaystyle\iint_D(12x-6y-2)\,dA=-2\cdot4\pi=-8\pi\) (the odd terms integrate to \(0\))
Line side. \(C:\ \vec r(t)=\langle2\cos t,2\sin t,0\rangle\). Compute \(\vec F\cdot\vec r\,{}'\)\(\langle4\sin t,\,0,\,6\cos t\rangle\cdot\langle-2\sin t,\,2\cos t,\,0\rangle=-8\sin^2t\), and \(\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi}-8\sin^2t\,dt=-8\pi\). Both sides agree.
332. \(\vec F=\langle xy,\,-z,\,0\rangle\), \(S\) the cube \(0\le x,y,z\le1\) without the face \(z=0\), outward. Find \(\iint_S\operatorname{curl}\vec F\cdot d\vec S\). What is the boundary \(C\)?The edge of the missing face: the unit square in the plane \(z=0\). The top has \(\vec N=\mathbf k\), so \(C\) is counterclockwise seen from above.
The line integral around \(C\)On \(z=0\): \(\vec F\cdot d\vec r=xy\,dx\). Only the side from \((1,1)\) to \((0,1)\) contributes: \(\displaystyle\int_1^0x\,dx=-\tfrac12\). So \(\iint_S\operatorname{curl}\vec F\cdot d\vec S=-\tfrac12\).
Check with surface independence: replace \(S\) by the bottom face \(z=0\) with \(\vec N=\mathbf k\).\(\operatorname{curl}\vec F=\langle1,0,-x\rangle\), so \(\displaystyle\iint_0^1\!\!\int_0^1(-x)\,dx\,dy=-\tfrac12\). Same value.
Pick the easier side
Exercises 334, 339, 346, 347
334. \(\vec F=\langle4y,\,z,\,2y\rangle\), \(S\) the part of \(x^2+y^2+z^2=4\) above \(z=0\), outward. Find \(\iint_S\operatorname{curl}\vec F\cdot d\vec S\).\(C\): \(\vec r=\langle2\cos t,2\sin t,0\rangle\), counterclockwise. On \(z=0\): \(\vec F\cdot d\vec r=4y\,dx=-16\sin^2t\,dt\), so the integral is \(-16\pi\). (Check: \(\operatorname{curl}\vec F=\langle1,0,-4\rangle\), flat disk upward: \(-4\cdot4\pi\).)
339. \(\vec F=\langle e^{xy}\cos z,\,x^2z,\,xy\rangle\), \(S\) the half sphere \(x=\sqrt{1-y^2-z^2}\), oriented toward the positive \(x\)-axis. Find \(\iint_S\operatorname{curl}\vec F\cdot d\vec S\).\(C\) is the circle \(y^2+z^2=1\) in the plane \(x=0\). There \(\vec F=\langle\cos z,0,0\rangle\) and \(d\vec r\) has no \(x\)-component, so \(\vec F\cdot d\vec r=0\). The integral is \(0\).
346. \(\vec F=\langle x,\,y^2,\,ze^{xy}\rangle\), \(S\) the part of \(z=1-x^2-2y^2\) with \(z\ge0\), upward. Find \(\iint_S\operatorname{curl}\vec F\cdot d\vec S\).\(C\) is the ellipse \(x^2+2y^2=1\) in the plane \(z=0\), where \(\vec F=\langle x,y^2,0\rangle=\nabla\big(\tfrac{x^2}{2}+\tfrac{y^3}{3}\big)\). A conservative field around a closed curve: \(0\).
347. \(\vec F=\langle z,3x,2z\rangle\), \(S:\ z=1-x^2-y^2\), \(z\ge0\), upward. Find \(\iint_S\operatorname{curl}\vec F\cdot d\vec S\).\(C\) is \(x^2+y^2=1\), \(z=0\), counterclockwise. \(\vec F\cdot d\vec r=3x\,dy=3\cos^2t\,dt\), so \(\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi}3\cos^2t\,dt=3\pi\).

Exercises 335, 343, 344 and two common mistakes
335. \(\displaystyle\oint_C\big[2xy^2z\,dx+2x^2yz\,dy+(x^2y^2-2z)\,dz\big]\), \(C:\ x=\cos t,\ y=\sin t,\ z=\sin t\), \(0\le t\le2\pi\).\(\vec F=\nabla(x^2y^2z-z^2)\) is conservative, so \(\operatorname{curl}\vec F=\vec 0\) and the integral is \(0\).
343. \(\iint_S\operatorname{curl}\vec F\cdot\vec N\,dS\), \(\vec F=\langle z^2,y^2,x\rangle\), \(S\) the triangle with vertices \((1,0,0)\), \((0,1,0)\), \((0,0,1)\), upward.\(\operatorname{curl}\vec F=\langle0,2z-1,0\rangle\). With \(z=1-x-y\), \(\vec t_x\times\vec t_y=\langle1,1,1\rangle\): \(\displaystyle\int_0^1\!\!\int_0^{1-x}(1-2x-2y)\,dy\,dx=-\dfrac16\).
344. \(\displaystyle\oint_C(z\,dx+x\,dy+y\,dz)\), \(C\) the triangle with vertices \((3,0,0)\), \((0,0,2)\), \((0,6,0)\) traversed in that order.\(\operatorname{curl}\vec F=\langle1,1,1\rangle\). The order of the vertices gives the normal \(\langle-12,-6,-18\rangle\) (pointing away from the first octant). The flux is \(\tfrac12\langle1,1,1\rangle\cdot\langle-12,-6,-18\rangle=-18\).
Spot the mistake. A student computes \(\iint_S\operatorname{curl}\vec F\cdot d\vec S\) for the upward-oriented dome \(S\) by taking the boundary circle \(C\) clockwise seen from above.With upward \(\vec N\), the positive direction of \(C\) is counterclockwise seen from above. Clockwise gives the opposite sign.
Spot the mistake. A student computes \(\iint_S\operatorname{curl}\vec F\cdot d\vec S\) over the whole unit sphere by using the equator as \(C\).The sphere is closed: it has no boundary. The equator is the boundary of a hemisphere. Over the whole sphere the integral is \(0\).
