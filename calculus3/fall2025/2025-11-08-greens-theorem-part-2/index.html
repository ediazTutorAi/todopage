Green's Theorem, Part 2

Green's Theorem
Circulation and flux forms, and the ingredients
Green's theorem

Let \(D\) be a region whose boundary \(C\) is a piecewise smooth, simple closed curve oriented counterclockwise, and let \(\mathbf F=\langle P,Q\rangle\) have continuous partial derivatives on \(D\). Then a line integral around \(C\) becomes a double integral over \(D\):

$$\textbf{Circulation (work):}\quad\oint_C P\,dx+Q\,dy=\iint_D (Q_x-P_y)\,dA$$ $$\textbf{Flux (outward):}\quad\oint_C\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf N\,ds=\iint_D (P_x+Q_y)\,dA$$
Ingredients: check these before using the theorem
  1. Is \(C\) closed? If not, close it with an easy piece (Step 7).
  2. Is it counterclockwise, with \(D\) on your left? If clockwise, put a minus sign (Step 8).
  3. Are \(P,Q\) smooth on all of \(D\)? A hole or a point where they blow up breaks the theorem.
  4. Which form? Work, circulation, \(\int P\,dx+Q\,dy\): use \(Q_x-P_y\). Flux, outward, across: use \(P_x+Q_y\).
  5. Can you describe \(D\)? Between two curves (\(dy\,dx\) or \(dx\,dy\)), a disk or ellipse (polar), a triangle (three lines).
The four moves, and the shortcuts to look for

1. Read off \(P\) and \(Q\).   2. Compute the integrand.   3. Describe \(D\) and set the limits.   4. Integrate, then fix the sign.

Integrand is a constant \(c\)\(c\cdot\text{Area}(D)\): disk \(\pi r^2\), ellipse \(\pi ab\), triangle \(\tfrac12bh\)
Integrand is \(0\) (\(P_y=Q_x\))the answer is \(0\) for every closed curve
Odd in \(x\) or \(y\) over a symmetric regionthe answer is \(0\)
\(x^2+y^2\) appears, \(D\) a diskpolar: \(r\,dr\,d\theta\)
Region Between Two Curves
Four moves
Problem

Use Green's theorem to evaluate \(\displaystyle\oint_C 11xy\,dx+(x+y)\,dy\), where \(C\) is the boundary of the region between \(y=0\) and \(y=121-x^2\).

Ingredients: closed? orientation? smooth?\(C\) is a boundary, so closed. No direction is stated, so take counterclockwise. \(P,Q\) are polynomials, smooth everywhere.
Move 1. Read off \(P\) and \(Q\).\(P=11xy,\ Q=x+y\)
Move 2. The integrand \(Q_x-P_y\).\(Q_x=1,\ P_y=11x\), so \(Q_x-P_y=1-11x\)
Move 3. Describe \(D\). Where do the curves meet?\(121-x^2=0\) at \(x=\pm11\). So \(-11\le x\le 11,\ 0\le y\le 121-x^2\)
Move 4. Set up and integrate.\(\displaystyle\int_{-11}^{11}\!\!\int_0^{121-x^2}(1-11x)\,dy\,dx=\int_{-11}^{11}(1-11x)(121-x^2)\,dx\)
Simplify before expanding: which part vanishes?\(11x(121-x^2)\) is odd, so it integrates to \(0\) on \([-11,11]\). What is left is the area: \(\displaystyle\int_{-11}^{11}(121-x^2)\,dx=2\Big(1331-\tfrac{1331}{3}\Big)=\tfrac{5324}{3}\)
Answer\(\tfrac{5324}{3}\)
A Constant Integrand
Four moves
Problem

Use Green's theorem to evaluate \(\displaystyle\oint_C (y-x)\,dx+(2x-y)\,dy\) for the path \(C\) defined by \(x=6\cos\theta,\ y=7\sin\theta\).

Ingredients: closed? orientation?\(\theta\) runs over \([0,2\pi]\), so the curve is closed, and \(\theta\) increasing means counterclockwise.
Move 1. \(P\) and \(Q\).\(P=y-x,\ Q=2x-y\)
Move 2. The integrand.\(Q_x=2,\ P_y=1\), so \(Q_x-P_y=1\), a constant
Move 3. What is \(D\)? What are its semi-axes?The ellipse \(\dfrac{x^2}{36}+\dfrac{y^2}{49}=1\), with \(a=6,\ b=7\)
Move 4. No integral to set up: constant times area.\(\displaystyle\iint_D 1\,dA=\pi ab=\pi(6)(7)=42\pi\)
Answer\(42\pi\)
Variation: the integrand were \(4\), same ellipse.\(4\cdot42\pi=168\pi\)
Variation: the same \(C\) traversed clockwise.\(-42\pi\)
Work Done Around a Triangle
Four moves
Problem

Use an appropriate form of Green's theorem to calculate the work done by \(\mathbf F(x,y)=\langle 18x^2+y,\ 19xy^2\rangle\) on a particle moving counterclockwise around the closed path \(C\), the boundary of the region between \(y=x,\ y=0,\ x=9\). Round to two decimal places.

Which form? The words give it away.Work is \(\oint\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r\), the circulation form: integrand \(Q_x-P_y\).
Move 1. \(P\) and \(Q\).\(P=18x^2+y,\ Q=19xy^2\)
Move 2. The integrand.\(Q_x=19y^2,\ P_y=1\), so \(Q_x-P_y=19y^2-1\)
Move 3. Sketch: the three lines meet where? Which order is easier?Vertices \((0,0),\ (9,0),\ (9,9)\). With \(dy\,dx\) there is one pair of limits: \(0\le x\le9,\ 0\le y\le x\).
Move 4a. Inner integral in \(y\).\(\displaystyle\int_0^x(19y^2-1)\,dy=\tfrac{19}{3}x^3-x\)
Move 4b. Outer integral in \(x\).\(\displaystyle\int_0^9\Big(\tfrac{19}{3}x^3-x\Big)dx=\tfrac{19}{12}(9^4)-\tfrac{81}{2}=\tfrac{41391}{4}\)
Answer, to two decimals\(10347.75\)
Why not the line integral directly?Three sides, three parametrizations, three integrals, versus one double integral.
A Disk and Polar Coordinates
Four moves
Problem

Use Green's theorem to evaluate \(\displaystyle\oint_C x^2y\,dx-xy^2\,dy\), where \(C\) is the circle \(x^2+y^2=9\) with counterclockwise orientation.

Move 1. \(P\) and \(Q\). Watch the minus sign in \(Q\).\(P=x^2y,\ Q=-xy^2\)
Move 2. The integrand.\(Q_x=-y^2,\ P_y=x^2\), so \(Q_x-P_y=-(x^2+y^2)\)
Move 3. A disk, with \(x^2+y^2\) in the integrand: which coordinates?Polar: \(x^2+y^2=r^2\), \(dA=r\,dr\,d\theta\), \(0\le r\le 3,\ 0\le\theta\le2\pi\)
Move 4. Set up and integrate.\(\displaystyle-\int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^3r^2\cdot r\,dr\,d\theta=-2\pi\cdot\tfrac{3^4}{4}=-\tfrac{81\pi}{2}\)
The same problem on a radius \(R\) circle.\(-2\pi\cdot\tfrac{R^4}{4}=-\tfrac{\pi R^4}{2}\)
The same with \(R=2\).\(-\tfrac{\pi\cdot16}{2}=-8\pi\)
Common slip: forgetting the factor \(r\) of \(dA\).\(\int_0^3r^2\,dr\) instead of \(\int_0^3r^3\,dr\) gives a wrong power of \(R\)
Spot the Zero
Four moves
Problem A

\(\displaystyle\oint_C (x^2-y^2)\,dx+10xy\,dy\), \(C:\ x^2+y^2=64\).

Move 2. The integrand.\(Q_x=10y,\ P_y=-2y\), so \(Q_x-P_y=12y\)
The disk is symmetric about the \(x\)-axis and \(12y\) is odd in \(y\). What does that say?The positive and negative halves cancel: the answer is \(0\) without integrating.
Check in polar.\(\displaystyle12\int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^8r\sin\theta\cdot r\,dr\,d\theta\) and \(\int_0^{2\pi}\sin\theta\,d\theta=0\)
Problem B

\(\displaystyle\oint_C e^x\cos(13y)\,dx-13e^x\sin(13y)\,dy\), where \(C\) is \(x^2+y^2=a^2\).

Move 2. The integrand.\(Q_x=-13e^x\sin(13y),\ P_y=-13e^x\sin(13y)\), so \(Q_x-P_y=0\)
What kind of field is this, and does the radius \(a\) matter?\(P_y=Q_x\): \(\mathbf F\) is conservative, with potential \(f=e^x\cos 13y\). So the answer is \(0\) for every closed curve, whatever \(a\) is.
Answer\(0\)
Open Paths: Close Them
Four moves
Problem

Find \(\displaystyle\int_C y^2\,dx+(2xy+x)\,dy\), where \(C\) is the arc of \(y=4-x^2\) from \((2,0)\) to \((-2,0)\).

Ingredient check: is \(C\) closed?No. The theorem does not apply yet.
Close it. Which return path is cheapest?\(C_2\): the segment from \((-2,0)\) to \((2,0)\) along the \(x\)-axis. There \(y=0\) and \(dy=0\). Then \(C+C_2\) is counterclockwise.
The integral along \(C_2\).\(\displaystyle\int_{C_2}y^2\,dx+(2xy+x)\,dy=0\), since \(y=0\) and \(dy=0\)
Green on the closed curve \(C+C_2\): \(Q_x-P_y=\)\((2y+1)-2y=1\), so \(\displaystyle\oint=\text{Area}(D)=\int_{-2}^2(4-x^2)\,dx=\tfrac{32}{3}\)
Subtract the piece you added.\(\displaystyle\int_C=\oint-\int_{C_2}=\tfrac{32}{3}-0=\tfrac{32}{3}\)
Check: parametrize \(x=t,\ y=4-t^2\), \(t\) from \(2\) to \(-2\).\(\displaystyle\int_2^{-2}\Big[(4-t^2)^2+\big(2t(4-t^2)+t\big)(-2t)\Big]dt=\tfrac{32}{3}\)
Orientation and Regions With Holes
Examples
Orientation of the boundary

Let \(D\) be a region and let \(C\) be a component of the boundary of \(D\). We say that \(C\) is positively oriented if, as we walk along \(C\) in the direction of orientation, region \(D\) is always on our left. Curve \(C\) is negatively oriented if, as we walk along \(C\) in the direction of orientation, region \(D\) is always on our right.

Let \(D\) be a region with finitely many holes and denote the boundary of \(D\) by \(\partial D\), with the inner holes given a negative orientation and the outer boundary a positive orientation. Then

$$\int_{\partial D}\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r=\iint_D (Q_x-P_y)\,dA\qquad\text{and}\qquad \int_{\partial D}\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf N\,ds=\iint_D (P_x+Q_y)\,dA.$$
\(\displaystyle\oint_{\partial D}\Big(\sin x-\frac{y^3}3\Big)dx+\Big(\frac{x^3}3+\sin y\Big)dy\), where \(D\) is the annulus \(1\le r\le2\), \(0\le\theta\le2\pi\).\(Q_x-P_y=x^2+y^2\); \(\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_1^2r^3\,dr\,d\theta=\tfrac{15\pi}2\)
\(\mathbf F=\Big\langle\dfrac{y}{x^2+y^2},\dfrac{-x}{x^2+y^2}\Big\rangle\) and \(C\) any simple closed curve oriented counterclockwise. What are the possible values of \(\displaystyle\int_C\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r\)?\(0\) if \(C\) does not encompass the origin; \(-2\pi\) if it does (\(\displaystyle\int_C=-\int_{C_1}\), \(C_1\) a clockwise circle about the origin inside \(C\))
\(\displaystyle\int_{\partial D}\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r\), \(D\) the annulus \(2\le r\le5\), \(\mathbf F(x,y)=\langle x^3,\ 5x+e^y\sin y\rangle\).\(Q_x-P_y=5\), area \(=21\pi\), so \(105\pi\)
Clockwise: \(\displaystyle\oint_C -y^3\,dx+x^3\,dy\), \(C:\ x^2+y^2=4\) clockwise. First the counterclockwise value.\(Q_x-P_y=3x^2+3y^2=3r^2\); \(3\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^2r^3\,dr\,d\theta=24\pi\). Clockwise reverses it: \(-24\pi\).
Which Form? Flux Across a Closed Curve
Examples
Choosing the form from the wording
work, circulation, \(\oint P\,dx+Q\,dy\), \(\oint\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r\)\(\displaystyle\iint_D(Q_x-P_y)\,dA\)
flux, outward, across \(C\), \(\oint\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf N\,ds\)\(\displaystyle\iint_D(P_x+Q_y)\,dA\)
The flux of \(\mathbf F(x,y)=\langle x,y\rangle\) across a circle of radius \(r\) centered at the origin.\(P_x+Q_y=2\), so \(2\iint_D dA=2\pi r^2\)
The flux of \(\mathbf F=\langle x^2+e^y,\ x+y\rangle\) across the triangle with vertices \((0,0)\), \((1,0)\), \((0,3)\) oriented clockwise.\(P_x+Q_y=2x+1\); clockwise gives a minus sign: \(-\displaystyle\int_0^1\!\!\int_0^{3-3x}(2x+1)\,dy\,dx=-\tfrac52\)
Water flows with velocity \(\mathbf v=\langle5x+y,\ x+3y\rangle\) m/sec. The amount flowing per second across the rectangle with vertices \((-1,-2)\), \((1,-2)\), \((1,3)\), \((-1,3)\), counterclockwise.\(P_x+Q_y=8\), so \(8\cdot(\text{area }10)=80\) m2/sec
The outward flux of \(\mathbf F=\langle x^2,y^2\rangle\) across the triangle with vertices \((0,0),(9,0),(9,9)\).\(P_x+Q_y=2x+2y\); \(\displaystyle\int_0^x(2x+2y)\,dy=3x^2\), so \(\int_0^9 3x^2\,dx=729\)
Area From a Boundary
Examples
Equation 6.14: area from the boundary

With \(\mathbf F=\langle-\tfrac y2,\tfrac x2\rangle\) we get \(Q_x-P_y=1\), so for a counterclockwise simple closed curve \(C\):

$$\text{area of }D=\tfrac12\oint_C -y\,dx+x\,dy.$$
The area enclosed by the ellipse \(x=a\cos t,\ y=b\sin t\), \(0\le t\le2\pi\).\(\tfrac12\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi}\big(-b\sin t\cdot(-a\sin t)+a\cos t\cdot b\cos t\big)dt=\tfrac12\int_0^{2\pi}ab\,dt=\pi ab\)
The area enclosed by \(\mathbf r(t)=\langle\sin t\cos t,\ \sin t\rangle\), \(0\le t\le\pi\).\(\tfrac12\displaystyle\int_0^\pi\sin^3t\,dt=\tfrac23\)
The area between the ellipse \(\dfrac{x^2}9+\dfrac{y^2}4=1\) and the circle \(x^2+y^2=25\).\(25\pi-6\pi=19\pi\)
The area of the pentagon with vertices \((0,4)\), \((4,1)\), \((3,0)\), \((-1,-1)\), \((-2,2)\).\(\tfrac12\oint -y\,dx+x\,dy\) over the five sides: \(17\) (this order of vertices is clockwise, so the sum is \(-17\))
Practice
Do the four moves on each
1. \(\displaystyle\oint_C 4xy\,dx+(2x^2+3x)\,dy\), \(C:\ x^2+y^2=25\) counterclockwise.\(Q_x-P_y=3\), so \(3\cdot25\pi=75\pi\)
2. \(\displaystyle\oint_C (e^x-y)\,dx+(x+\sin y)\,dy\), \(C\) the triangle \((0,0),(4,0),(0,3)\) counterclockwise.\(Q_x-P_y=2\), so \(2\cdot6=12\)
3. \(\displaystyle\oint_C \big(3y-e^{\sin x}\big)dx+\big(7x+\sqrt{y^4+1}\big)dy\), \(C:\ x^2+y^2=9\) counterclockwise.\(Q_x-P_y=7-3=4\), so \(4\cdot9\pi=36\pi\)
4. \(\displaystyle\oint_C xy\,dx+x^2\,dy\), \(C\) the boundary of the region between \(y=x^2\) and \(y=x\), counterclockwise.\(Q_x-P_y=2x-x=x\); \(\displaystyle\int_0^1\!\!\int_{x^2}^{x}x\,dy\,dx=\int_0^1(x^2-x^3)dx=\tfrac1{12}\)
5. The work of \(\mathbf F=\langle x,\ x^3+3xy^2\rangle\) on a particle going along the \(x\)-axis from \((-2,0)\) to \((2,0)\), then back along the upper semicircle .\(Q_x-P_y=3(x^2+y^2)\); \(3\displaystyle\int_0^\pi\!\!\int_0^2r^3\,dr\,d\theta=12\pi\)
6. \(\displaystyle\oint_C2xy\,dx+(x+y)\,dy\), \(C\) the boundary of the region between \(y=0\) and \(y=4-x^2\).\(\displaystyle\int_{-2}^{2}(1-2x)(4-x^2)\,dx=\tfrac{32}3\)
7. \(\displaystyle\oint_Cxy\,dx+(x+y)\,dy\), \(C\) the boundary of the region between \(x^2+y^2=1\) and \(x^2+y^2=9\).\(Q_x-P_y=1-x\); \(8\pi-0=8\pi\)
8. \(\displaystyle\oint_C(y+x)\,dx+(x+\sin y)\,dy\), \(C\) any smooth simple closed curve.\(Q_x-P_y=1-1=0\), so \(0\)
9. The outward flux of \(\mathbf F=-x\,\mathbf i+2y\,\mathbf j\) over the square with corners \((\pm1,\pm1)\).\(P_x+Q_y=1\); area \(4\), so \(4\)
10. The outward flux of \(\mathbf F=\langle x^3,y^3\rangle\) across the boundary of \([0,2]\times[0,2]\).\(P_x+Q_y=3x^2+3y^2\); \(\displaystyle\int_0^2\!\!\int_0^2(3x^2+3y^2)\,dx\,dy=32\)
