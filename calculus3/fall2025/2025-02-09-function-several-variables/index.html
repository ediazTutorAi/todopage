Functions of Several Variables

MATH 2326 · Dr. Esteban Diaz
Definition
Function of Two Variables
A function of two variables $z=f(x,y)$ maps each ordered pair $(x,y)$ in a subset $D$ of the real plane $\mathbb{R}^2$ to a unique real number $z$. The set D is called the domain of the function. The range of f is the set of all real number z that has at least one ordered pair $(x,y)$ in D such that $f(x,y)=z$.
Domains and Ranges
Two Worked Examples

(a) $f(x,y) = 3x+5y+2$

What are the domain and range?

Domain: all of $\mathbb{R}^2$ (linear in $x$ and $y$, no restrictions). Range: all of $\mathbb{R}$.

(b) $g(x,y) = \sqrt{9-x^2-y^2}$

What are the domain and range?

The expression under the square root must be nonnegative: $9-x^2-y^2 \ge 0$, i.e. $x^2+y^2 \le 9$ — the closed disk of radius 3 centered at the origin. Since $g$ outputs a square root, the range is $[0,3]$.

Graphing Functions of Two Variables
Surfaces in Three Dimensions
The graph of $z=f(x,y)$ is the surface formed by the set of all points $(x,y,f(x,y))$ for $(x,y)$ in the domain.
$g(x,y) = \sqrt{9-x^2-y^2}$ — a hemisphere
g(x,y)=sqrt(9-x^2-y^2)
$f(x,y) = x^2+y^2$ — a paraboloid
f(x,y)=x^2+y^2
A profit function

A firm's profit (in thousands of dollars) from selling two products is modeled by $f(x,y) = 16-(x-3)^2-(y-2)^2$, where $x$ and $y$ are the amounts (in hundreds of units) of each product sold. Where is profit maximized, and what is the maximum profit?

Both squared terms are minimized (at 0) when $x=3$ and $y=2$, giving the maximum $f(3,2)=16$. The vertex of this downward paraboloid is $(3,2,16)$.

f(x,y)=16-(x-3)^2-(y-2)^2 A=(3,2,f(3,2))
Level Curves
Definition
Given a function $f(x,y)$ and a number $c$ in the range of $f$, a level curve of $f$ for the value $c$ is the set of points satisfying $f(x,y)=c$. Stacking level curves for evenly spaced values of $c$ produces a contour map — the same idea as elevation lines on a topographic map.
Level curves of $g(x,y)=\sqrt{9-x^2-y^2}$

For $c=0,1,2,3$ the level curves $\sqrt{9-x^2-y^2}=c$ are the concentric circles $x^2+y^2=9-c^2$.

x^2+y^2=9 x^2+y^2=8 x^2+y^2=5 x^2+y^2=0
Worked example

Find the level curve of $f(x,y) = 8+8x-4y-4x^2-y^2$ at $c=0$.

Set $8+8x-4y-4x^2-y^2=0$ and complete the square in $x$ and $y$: $$\frac{(x-1)^2}{4} + \frac{(y+2)^2}{16} = 1$$ an ellipse centered at $(1,-2)$.

(x-1)^2/4+(y+2)^2/16=1
Vertical Traces
Definition
A vertical trace of $z=f(x,y)$ is the curve obtained by intersecting the graph of $f$ with a plane parallel to the $xz$- or $yz$-plane: fixing $x=a$ gives the trace solving $f(a,y)=z$, and fixing $y=b$ gives the trace solving $f(x,b)=z$.
Worked example: $f(x,y) = \sin(x)\cos(y)$

What is the trace at $y=0$? At $x=0$?

At $y=0$: $f(x,0)=\sin(x)\cos(0)=\sin(x)$ — a sine curve in the $xz$-plane.
At $x=0$: $f(0,y)=\sin(0)\cos(y)=0$ — the trace is identically 0, the $y$-axis itself.

f(x,y)=sin(x)cos(y) p:y=0 Intersect(f,p)
Functions of More than Two Variables
A function of three variables is a function of the form $w=f(x,y,z)$. The same definitions of domain and range apply in this case.
Domain examples

(a) $f(x,y,z) = \dfrac{3x-4y+2z}{\sqrt{9-x^2-y^2-z^2}}$

Need $9-x^2-y^2-z^2 > 0$ (strictly, since it's in a denominator): $x^2+y^2+z^2 < 9$ — the open ball of radius 3 centered at the origin.

(b) $g(x,y,t) = \dfrac{\sqrt{2t-4}}{x^2-y^2}$

Need $2t-4 \ge 0 \Rightarrow t \ge 2$, and the denominator nonzero: $x^2-y^2 \ne 0 \Rightarrow y \ne \pm x$.

Level Surfaces
Definition
Given a function $f(x,y,z)$ and a number $c$ in the range of $f$, a level surface of $f$ is the set of points satisfying $f(x,y,z)=c$.
Worked example

Describe the level surface of $f(x,y,z) = 4x^2+9y^2-z^2$ at $c=1$.

$4x^2+9y^2-z^2=1$ is a hyperboloid of one sheet, opening along the $z$-axis.

4x^2+9y^2-z^2=1
Quick Check
Try these before moving on

1. Find the domain of $f(x,y) = \sqrt{36-9x^2-9y^2}$.

Need $36-9x^2-9y^2 \ge 0 \Rightarrow x^2+y^2 \le 4$ — the closed disk of radius 2.

2. Find the level curve of $g(x,y) = x^2+y^2-6x+2y$ at $c=15$.

Complete the square: $(x-3)^2+(y+1)^2=25$ — a circle of radius 5 centered at $(3,-1)$.
