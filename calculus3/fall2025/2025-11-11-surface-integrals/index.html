Surface Integrals

Parametric Surfaces
Definition, Example 6.58 and Checkpoint 6.47

A parameterized surface is given by a description of the form $$\vec r(u,v)=\langle x(u,v),\,y(u,v),\,z(u,v)\rangle.$$ The parameters \(u\) and \(v\) vary over a region in the \(uv\)-plane called the parameter domain.

Example 6.58: describe the surface \(S\) parameterized by \(\vec r(u,v)=\langle\cos u,\sin u,v\rangle\), \(-\infty<u<\infty\), \(-\infty<v<\infty\). The blue curves hold \(u\) constant, the orange curves hold \(v\) constant.

Hold \(u=K\). The curve is \(\langle\cos K,\sin K,v\rangle\).A vertical line through \((\cos K,\sin K)\) in the \(xy\)-plane.
Hold \(v=K\). The curve is \(\langle\cos u,\sin u,K\rangle\).A circle in the plane \(z=K\) with radius 1 and center \((0,0,K)\). So \(S\) is the cylinder \(x^2+y^2=1\).
Cylinder \(x^2+y^2=R^2\)\(\vec r(u,v)=\langle R\cos u,R\sin u,v\rangle\), \(0\le u<2\pi\), \(-\infty<v<\infty\)
Restricting the domain to \(0\le u\le\pi\), \(0<v<6\)A half-cylinder of height 6.
Checkpoint 6.47. Describe \(\vec r(u,v)=\langle2\cos u,2\sin u,v\rangle\), \(0\le u<2\pi\), \(-\infty<v<\infty\).The cylinder \(x^2+y^2=4\).

Example 6.59: \(\vec r(u,v)=\langle u\cos v,u\sin v,u^2\rangle\), \(0\le u<\infty\), \(0\le v<2\pi\).

Spheres, Graphs and Regular Parameterizations
Definitions and Checkpoint 6.50

The sphere of radius \(\rho\) centered at the origin: $$\vec r(\varphi,\theta)=\langle\rho\cos\theta\sin\varphi,\ \rho\sin\theta\sin\varphi,\ \rho\cos\varphi\rangle,\quad 0\le\theta\le2\pi,\ 0\le\varphi\le\pi.$$ The graph of \(z=f(x,y)\): \(\vec r(x,y)=\langle x,y,f(x,y)\rangle\). Likewise \(\vec r(y,z)=\langle g(y,z),y,z\rangle\) for \(x=g(y,z)\) and \(\vec r(x,z)=\langle x,h(x,z),z\rangle\) for \(y=h(x,z)\).

Definition: regular (smooth) parameterization

Parameterization \(\vec r(u,v)=\langle x(u,v),y(u,v),z(u,v)\rangle\) is a regular parameterization if \(\vec r_u\times\vec r_v\) is not zero for any point \((u,v)\) in the parameter domain. It is smooth if the vector \(\vec r_u\times\vec r_v\) is not zero for any choice of \(u\) and \(v\) in the parameter domain.

Example: \(\vec r(u,v)=\langle0,\cos v,1\rangle\) has \(\vec r_u=\vec 0\), so \(\vec r_u\times\vec r_v=\vec 0\) and its image is only a line, not a surface.

Checkpoint 6.50. Is \(\vec r(u,v)=\langle u^2v,\ v+1,\ \sin u\rangle\), \(0\le u\le2\), \(0\le v\le3\), smooth?\(\vec r_u=\langle2uv,0,\cos u\rangle\), \(\vec r_v=\langle u^2,1,0\rangle\), so \(\vec r_u\times\vec r_v=\langle-\cos u,\ u^2\cos u,\ 2uv\rangle\). At \(u=\pi/2\), \(v=0\) this is \(\vec 0\). Not smooth.
Surface Area of a Parametric Surface
Definition, Example 6.62 and Checkpoint 6.51

A small piece of the surface is approximated by the parallelogram spanned by \(\Delta u\,\vec t_u\) and \(\Delta v\,\vec t_v\), so $$\Delta S_{ij}\approx\|\vec t_u(P_{ij})\times\vec t_v(P_{ij})\|\,\Delta u\,\Delta v.$$

Definition: surface area

Let \(\vec r(u,v)=\langle x(u,v),y(u,v),z(u,v)\rangle\) with parameter domain \(D\) be a smooth parameterization of surface \(S\), traced out only once as \((u,v)\) varies over \(D\). The surface area of \(S\) is $$\iint_D\|\vec t_u\times\vec t_v\|\,dA,\qquad \vec t_u=\Big\langle\frac{\partial x}{\partial u},\frac{\partial y}{\partial u},\frac{\partial z}{\partial u}\Big\rangle,\ \vec t_v=\Big\langle\frac{\partial x}{\partial v},\frac{\partial y}{\partial v},\frac{\partial z}{\partial v}\Big\rangle.$$

The cone of Example 6.62 with \(r=2\), \(h=3\) (\(k=r/h\)). Slide the number of pieces up: the sum of the parallelogram areas approaches the area.

Example 6.62. Lateral surface area of the right circular cone with height \(h\) and radius \(r\). Parameterize it, with \(k=\tan\alpha=r/h\).\(\vec s(u,v)=\langle kv\cos u,\ kv\sin u,\ v\rangle\), \(0\le u<2\pi\), \(0\le v\le h\)
\(\vec t_u\times\vec t_v\) and its magnitude\(\vec t_u=\langle-kv\sin u,kv\cos u,0\rangle\), \(\vec t_v=\langle k\cos u,k\sin u,1\rangle\), \(\vec t_u\times\vec t_v=\langle kv\cos u,\ kv\sin u,\ -k^2v\rangle\), and \(\|\vec t_u\times\vec t_v\|=kv\sqrt{1+k^2}\).
The area\(\displaystyle\int_0^h\!\!\int_0^{2\pi}kv\sqrt{1+k^2}\,du\,dv=\pi kh^2\sqrt{1+k^2}=\pi r\sqrt{h^2+r^2}\)
Checkpoint 6.51. Area of \(\vec r(u,v)=\langle u+v,\ u^2,\ 2v\rangle\), \(0\le u\le3\), \(0\le v\le2\).\(\vec r_u\times\vec r_v=\langle4u,-2,-2u\rangle\), \(\|\cdot\|=2\sqrt{5u^2+1}\). Area \(=4\displaystyle\int_0^3\sqrt{5u^2+1}\,du=6\sqrt{46}+\dfrac{2}{\sqrt5}\sinh^{-1}(3\sqrt5)\approx43.02\)
Surface Area: Sphere and Surfaces of Revolution
Examples 6.63, 6.64 and Checkpoints 6.52, 6.53
Example 6.63. Show that the sphere \(x^2+y^2+z^2=r^2\) has area \(4\pi r^2\).\(\vec t_\varphi\times\vec t_\theta=\langle r^2\cos\theta\sin^2\varphi,\ r^2\sin\theta\sin^2\varphi,\ r^2\sin\varphi\cos\varphi\rangle\), \(\|\cdot\|=r^2\sin\varphi\). Area \(=\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^\pi r^2\sin\varphi\,d\varphi\,d\theta=4\pi r^2\).
Checkpoint 6.52. Show that the cylinder \(x^2+y^2=r^2\), \(0\le z\le h\), has area \(2\pi rh\).\(\vec r(u,v)=\langle r\cos u,r\sin u,v\rangle\), \(\|\vec t_u\times\vec t_v\|=r\). Area \(=\displaystyle\int_0^h\!\!\int_0^{2\pi}r\,du\,dv=2\pi rh\).

Surface of revolution: rotate \(y=f(x)\ge0\), \(a\le x\le b\), about the \(x\)-axis: $$\vec r(x,\theta)=\langle x,\ f(x)\cos\theta,\ f(x)\sin\theta\rangle,\quad a\le x\le b,\ 0\le\theta<2\pi.$$

Example 6.64. Area of the surface obtained by rotating \(y=x^2\), \(0\le x\le b\), about the \(x\)-axis.\(\vec t_x\times\vec t_\theta=\langle2x^3,-x^2\cos\theta,-x^2\sin\theta\rangle\), \(\|\cdot\|=x^2\sqrt{4x^2+1}\). Area \(=2\pi\displaystyle\int_0^bx^2\sqrt{4x^2+1}\,dx=\dfrac{\pi}{32}\Big[2b(8b^2+1)\sqrt{4b^2+1}-\sinh^{-1}(2b)\Big]\)
Checkpoint 6.53. Area of the surface obtained by rotating \(y=\sin x\), \(0\le x\le\pi\), about the \(x\)-axis.\(\|\vec t_x\times\vec t_\theta\|=\sin x\sqrt{1+\cos^2x}\). Area \(=2\pi\displaystyle\int_0^\pi\sin x\sqrt{1+\cos^2x}\,dx=2\pi\big(\sqrt2+\sinh^{-1}1\big)\approx14.42\)
Surface Integral of a Scalar-Valued Function
Equation 6.19, Examples 6.65, 6.66 and Checkpoint 6.54
Surface integral of a scalar-valued function

$$\iint_Sf(x,y,z)\,dS=\lim_{m,n\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^m\sum_{j=1}^nf(P_{ij})\,\Delta S_{ij}=\iint_Df(\vec r(u,v))\,\|\vec t_u\times\vec t_v\|\,dA.$$

Compare with the scalar line integral \(\int_Cf\,ds=\int_a^bf(\vec r(t))\|\vec r'(t)\|\,dt\). Example 6.65: \(\iint_S5\,dS\) over \(\vec r(u,v)=\langle u,u^2,v\rangle\), \(0\le u\le2\), \(0\le v\le u\) (a triangular parameter domain).

Example 6.65. Compute \(\iint_S5\,dS\).\(\vec t_u\times\vec t_v=\langle2u,-1,0\rangle\), \(\|\cdot\|=\sqrt{1+4u^2}\). \(5\displaystyle\int_0^2\!\!\int_0^u\sqrt{1+4u^2}\,dv\,du=5\int_0^2u\sqrt{1+4u^2}\,du=\dfrac{5(17^{3/2}-1)}{12}\approx28.79\)
Example 6.66. \(\iint_S(x+y^2)\,dS\), \(S\) the cylinder \(x^2+y^2=4\), \(0\le z\le3\).\(\vec r(u,v)=\langle2\cos u,2\sin u,v\rangle\), \(\|\vec t_u\times\vec t_v\|=2\). \(\displaystyle\int_0^3\!\!\int_0^{2\pi}(2\cos u+4\sin^2u)\,2\,du\,dv=24\pi\)
Checkpoint 6.54. \(\iint_S(x^2-z)\,dS\), \(\vec r(u,v)=\langle v,\ u^2+v^2,\ 1\rangle\), \(0\le u\le2\), \(0\le v\le3\).\(\vec r_u\times\vec r_v=\langle0,0,-2u\rangle\), \(\|\cdot\|=2u\). \(\displaystyle\int_0^3\!\!\int_0^2(v^2-1)\,2u\,du\,dv=24\)
Piecewise Smooth Surfaces and Mass of a Sheet
Examples 6.67, 6.68 and Checkpoints 6.55, 6.56

If a thin sheet of metal has the shape of surface \(S\) and density \(\rho(x,y,z)\), its mass is \(m=\iint_S\rho(x,y,z)\,dS\). Example 6.68: the sheet \(z=1+x+2y\) over \(0\le x\le4\), \(0\le y\le2\), with \(\rho=x^2yz\).

Example 6.67. \(\iint_Sz^2\,dS\), where \(S\) is the piece of the sphere \(x^2+y^2+z^2=4\) on or above \(z=1\) together with the disk enclosed by the plane \(z=1\) and the sphere.\(S\) is piecewise smooth: \(S_1\) the disk, \(S_2\) the spherical cap. \(S_1\): \(x^2+y^2=3\) at \(z=1\), \(\vec r=\langle u\cos v,u\sin v,1\rangle\), \(0\le u\le\sqrt3\), \(\|\vec t_u\times\vec t_v\|=u\), so \(\iint_{S_1}z^2\,dS=2\pi\int_0^{\sqrt3}u\,du=3\pi\). \(S_2\): \(0\le\varphi\le\pi/3\), \(\|\vec t_\varphi\times\vec t_\theta\|=4\sin\varphi\), so \(\iint_{S_2}z^2\,dS=2\pi\int_0^{\pi/3}16\cos^2\varphi\sin\varphi\,d\varphi=\dfrac{28\pi}{3}\). Total \(\dfrac{37\pi}{3}\).
Checkpoint 6.55. \(\iint_S(x-y)\,dS\), \(S\) the cylinder \(x^2+y^2=1\), \(0\le z\le2\), including the circular top and bottom.\(0\). Each of the three pieces gives \(0\), since \(x-y\) is odd under \((x,y)\to(-x,-y)\) and each piece is symmetric.
Example 6.68. The mass of the sheet.\(\vec t_x\times\vec t_y=\langle-1,-2,1\rangle\), \(\|\cdot\|=\sqrt6\). \(m=\sqrt6\displaystyle\int_0^4\!\!\int_0^2x^2y(1+x+2y)\,dy\,dx=\dfrac{2560\sqrt6}{9}\approx696.74\)
Checkpoint 6.56. Mass of the paraboloid \(z=x^2+y^2\), \(0\le z\le4\), with density \(\rho(x,y,z)=z+1\).\(\vec r=\langle r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta,r^2\rangle\), \(\|\cdot\|=r\sqrt{1+4r^2}\), \(0\le r\le2\). \(m=2\pi\displaystyle\int_0^2(r^2+1)\,r\sqrt{1+4r^2}\,dr=\dfrac{\pi(187\sqrt{17}-3)}{20}\approx120.64\)
Orientation of a Surface
Example 6.69 and Checkpoint 6.57

For any point on a smooth surface \(S\) there are two unit normal vectors \(\vec N\) and \(-\vec N\). If it is possible to choose a unit normal \(\vec N\) at every point so that \(\vec N\) varies continuously over \(S\), then \(S\) is orientable. A closed surface with the outward normal has the positive orientation. For a parameterization, the choice $$\vec N=\frac{\vec t_u\times\vec t_v}{\|\vec t_u\times\vec t_v\|}$$ gives an orientation of \(S\). For \(z=f(x,y)\): \(\vec t_x\times\vec t_y=\langle-f_x,-f_y,1\rangle\), the upward normal.

The Möbius strip (a half-twist, ends glued) has only one side: it is nonorientable, so no vector surface integral is defined over it.

Example 6.69. An orientation of the cylinder \(x^2+y^2=r^2\), \(0\le z\le h\).\(\vec r(u,v)=\langle r\cos u,r\sin u,v\rangle\), \(\vec t_u\times\vec t_v=\langle r\cos u,r\sin u,0\rangle\), \(\|\cdot\|=r\). \(\vec N(u,v)=\langle\cos u,\sin u,0\rangle\): all vectors point outward, an outward orientation.
Checkpoint 6.57. The upward orientation of the graph of \(f(x,y)=xy\).\(\vec t_x\times\vec t_y=\langle-y,-x,1\rangle\), so \(\vec N=\dfrac{\langle-y,-x,1\rangle}{\sqrt{x^2+y^2+1}}\)
Surface Integral of a Vector Field
Equations 6.20, 6.21, Example 6.70 and Checkpoint 6.58
Definition: surface integral of a vector field (flux)

Let \(\vec F\) be a continuous vector field with a domain that contains oriented surface \(S\) with unit normal vector \(\vec N\). The surface integral of \(\vec F\) over \(S\) is $$\iint_S\vec F\cdot d\vec S=\iint_S\vec F\cdot\vec N\,dS.$$ It is also called the flux of \(\vec F\) across \(S\). If \(S\) is parameterized by \(\vec r(u,v)\) over \(D\), $$\iint_S\vec F\cdot\vec N\,dS=\iint_D\big(\vec F(\vec r(u,v))\cdot(\vec t_u\times\vec t_v)\big)\,dA.$$

Example 6.70: \(\vec F=\langle-y,x,0\rangle\) on \(\vec r(u,v)=\langle u,\ v^2-u,\ u+v\rangle\), \(0\le u<3\), \(0\le v\le4\). Green arrows are the unit normal \(\vec N\), orange arrows are \(\vec F\). Flip the orientation and the flux changes sign.

Example 6.70. Compute \(\iint_S\vec F\cdot\vec N\,dS\).\(\vec t_u=\langle1,-1,1\rangle\), \(\vec t_v=\langle0,2v,1\rangle\), \(\vec t_u\times\vec t_v=\langle-1-2v,-1,2v\rangle\). \(\vec F(\vec r)=\langle u-v^2,u,0\rangle\). \(\displaystyle\int_0^4\!\!\int_0^3\big[(u-v^2)(-1-2v)-u\big]\,du\,dv=\int_0^4(6v^3+3v^2-9v-9)\,dv=340\)
Checkpoint 6.58. \(\iint_S\vec F\cdot d\vec S\), \(\vec F=\langle0,-z,y\rangle\), \(S\) the part of the unit sphere in the first octant, outward orientation.On the unit sphere \(\vec t_\varphi\times\vec t_\theta=\sin\varphi\,\langle x,y,z\rangle\), so \(\vec F\cdot(\vec t_\varphi\times\vec t_\theta)=\sin\varphi\,(-zy+yz)=0\). The flux is \(0\) (\(\vec F\) is tangent to the sphere).
Mass Flux, Flow Rate and Heat Flow
Examples 6.71, 6.72 and Checkpoint 6.59

For a fluid with velocity \(\vec v\) and density \(\rho\): mass flux across \(S\) is \(\iint_S\rho\vec v\cdot d\vec S\), and the flow rate (volume per time) is \(\iint_S\vec v\cdot d\vec S\). Heat flow: \(\vec F=-k\nabla T\), with rate \(\iint_S-k\nabla T\cdot d\vec S\).

Example 6.71: \(\vec v=\langle2x,2y,z\rangle\), density \(80\ \text{kg/m}^3\), \(S\) the hemisphere \(x^2+y^2+z^2=9\), \(z\ge0\), oriented outward.

Example 6.71. The mass flow rate across \(S\).\(\vec v(\vec r)\cdot(\vec t_\varphi\times\vec t_\theta)=54\sin^3\varphi+27\cos^2\varphi\sin\varphi=54\sin\varphi-27\cos^2\varphi\sin\varphi\). \(80\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_0^{\pi/2}\big(54\sin\varphi-27\cos^2\varphi\sin\varphi\big)\,d\varphi\,d\theta=80\cdot2\pi\cdot45=7200\pi\). Without the density, the flow rate is \(90\pi\ \text{m}^3/\text{s}\).
Checkpoint 6.59. \(\vec v=\langle x^2+y^2,\ z,\ 4y\rangle\) m/s, density \(100\ \text{kg/m}^3\), \(S\) the half-cylinder \(\vec r(u,v)=\langle\cos u,\sin u,v\rangle\), \(0\le u\le\pi\), \(0\le v\le2\), outward. Mass flux?\(\vec t_u\times\vec t_v=\langle\cos u,\sin u,0\rangle\), \(\vec v\cdot(\vec t_u\times\vec t_v)=\cos u+v\sin u\). \(100\displaystyle\int_0^2\!\!\int_0^\pi(\cos u+v\sin u)\,du\,dv=100\cdot4=400\)
Example 6.72. Cast iron (\(k=55\)), solid cylinder \(x^2+y^2\le1\), \(1\le z\le4\), \(T=(x^2+y^2)z\). Heat flow across the boundary, oriented outward?\(\nabla T=\langle2xz,2yz,x^2+y^2\rangle\). Bottom (\(\vec N=-\mathbf k\)): \(55\pi/2\). Top (\(\vec N=\mathbf k\)): \(-55\pi/2\). Side (\(\vec N=\langle x,y,0\rangle\), \(\nabla T\cdot\vec N=2z\)): \(-55\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi}\!\!\int_1^42z\,dz\,d\theta=-1650\pi\). Total \(-1650\pi\).
