Volumes by Slicing, Disk, and washers

Volume by Slicing or Cross-Section
Let S be a solid extending from \(x=a\) to \(x=b\). Suppose that every plane is perpendicular to the x-axis intersects Sin a cross section whose are can be given by a function \(A(x)\). Then the volume of the solid is obtained by summing the volumes of infinitely many infinitesimally thin slices. \[V=\int_a^b A(x)dx\]We can interpret this formula as the sum of infinitesimal volumes of cylinders, these cylinders are construct by taking a cross sectional area of the solid times the thickness of the section to create a cylinder.
Disk and Washer Methods
If the sectional area is given by a disk or a washer, then we name this method of computing the volume of the corresponding solid as disk or washwer methods
